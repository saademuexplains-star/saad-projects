Every night, Elsbeth sang the same lullaby over her children’s beds. Her voice was cracked and raw, and her husband begged her to rest. She refused. He noticed she never looked at the children while she sang. She stared at the floorboards.
One winter she caught a fever and lost her voice entirely. Her husband told her not to worry and sat down on the bed to sing it for her. He knew the tune well enough, and he hummed it as best he could.
The house went quiet. The children’s breathing changed. And beneath the bed, something began to shift, slow and heavy, like a sleeper turning over.
Elsbeth tore herself up from her pillow, silent and white-faced, and clamped her hand over his mouth.
She had never been singing the children to sleep. She’d been singing to the thing that slept beneath them.
