Your boss hands you a paintbrush dipped in glowing paint and tells you to put it in your mouth. It's 1917. You're 19, painting numbers on soldiers' watches in New Jersey. Lip, dip, paint, hundreds of times a day, because the company swears it's safe enough to eat. So you paint your nails with it, your teeth, you wear your best dress to work so it'll glow at the dance. Then a tooth falls out, then another. The dentist touches your jaw, and it comes away in his hand. And all this time, in the company's own lab, chemists handle the same paint with lead aprons, masks and tongs. You die at 24, and your death certificate says syphilis. Five years later, with your friends dying the same way, they dig you up, lift the lid, and your body is still glowing, because the paint was radium. And you were one of the Radium Girls.
