# The Radium Girls - beat sheet (VO `_vo19/vo_trim.wav`, 44.64 s; pauses capped at 0.20 s)

Story: Orange, New Jersey, 1917. You paint glowing numbers on soldiers' watch dials with radium paint ("Undark"),
pointing the brush with your lips - "lip, dip, paint" - because the company swears it's safe. Your teeth fall out, your
jaw crumbles in the dentist's hand, while the company's chemists handle the same paint behind lead. You die at 24;
the certificate says syphilis. Five years later they dig you up and your body still glows. Loop: the coffin's glow
becomes the glowing brush tip of frame 0. The ending holds ~2 s on the last image before the loop morph.

Frames: 44.64 s of voice + 2.0 s hold + 0.5 s loop morph = 47.15 s -> 2829 frames at 60 fps.

## Hand and arm rules for this film (the user: "PLEASE NO HANDS AND ARMS MISTAKES IN THIS ONE")
Every hand on screen is one of these proven set-ups, checked at full size and 3x on the joints:
1. **Forearm level across the body** (elbow hanging at the side, hand in front of the belly or on the table) - the
   pose that read cleanly in Coca-Cola v2. Never a hand raised to the face or the chest (the forearm folds back over
   the upper arm), never a forearm pointing at the lens (a stub with the cuff on the elbow).
2. **Arm hanging** at the side, hand hidden behind the table or furniture (the far side from the key light).
3. **Inserts with no arm in frame**: the brush alone (held from out of frame), a palm-up hand whose forearm leaves the
   frame edge, glowing nails in the dark (only the glow reads).
4. Furniture in front of an arm is masked round the WHOLE arm (`V.behindAlso`, armRfull); lights inside furniture are
   clipped to the furniture; a held prop and its fist are lit in the body's one pass; no contact shadow (`armAO`) on
   white sleeves over dark clothes.
