You're thirty years old, a mother of five, when doctors find a tumor growing inside you. The only hospital nearby that treats Black patients is Johns Hopkins, and while you lie asleep on the table, a surgeon quietly cuts off a piece of your tumor and sends it down the hall to a lab. Nobody asks you. Nobody tells you. Your cancer spreads fast, and a few months later, at just thirty-one, you die, leaving your children behind. But in that lab, something impossible is happening. Every human cell scientists have ever tried to grow dies within days. Yours don't. They double every single day, and they never stop. Soon they're shipped all over the world, helping create the polio vaccine, cancer treatments and medicines for millions. And today, more than seventy years after your funeral, your cells are still alive, still growing in labs around the world, under the name HeLa, for Henrietta Lacks.
