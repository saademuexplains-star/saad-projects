You drink a cup of cool water in an ancient market, completely unaware you just swallowed a microscopic predator. For twelve months, you feel nothing, while a three-foot-long white worm quietly grows inside your torso, eating its way down toward your leg. One morning, your ankle feels like it's being pressed against red-hot iron. Your skin bubbles into a massive blister, and when you step into a cold river to stop the burning, a white head bursts straight through your flesh. You grab it to yank it out, but the village healer slaps your hand away. If that worm snaps inside your leg, its internal venom will rot your blood and kill you by sunrise. Instead, the healer ties the tip of the worm to a tiny wooden twig and turns it just one inch. You have to sit there and let him wind that worm out of your raw flesh, millimeter by millimeter, for the next thirty days and that worm coiled around a stick became the exact symbol painted on every ambulance today.
