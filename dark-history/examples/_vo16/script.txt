On a summer morning, a stranger in a coat of many colours walks into your town playing a strange tune on a pipe, and every child who hears it stops what they're doing and starts to follow him. You're a mother in a small German town, and from your window you watch your own son walk past, smiling, not even turning his head when you scream his name. You run after them, but they're already through the town gate and climbing toward the hills, a hundred and thirty children dancing behind the music. By the time you reach the top, the music has stopped, and they're gone. No bodies. No footprints. Nothing. Only three are left behind: one too lame to keep up, one deaf who never heard the song, and one blind who couldn't see where they went. Centuries later, the world turns it into a bedtime story. But the children were real. Your son was real. And the stranger who took them is still known everywhere as the Pied Piper of Hamelin.
