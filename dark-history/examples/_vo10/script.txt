Every day for ten days a needle filled with rabies is pushed into your stomach, each dose made from the spinal cord of a rabbit that died of the disease, stronger than the day before, and the man ordering it isn't even a doctor. You're nine years old, and two days before the first shot a mad dog ripped into you fourteen times, and in your world every person bitten by a rabid dog dies the same way, burning with fever, foaming at the mouth, screaming at the sight of water. No human has ever been given these shots before, and on the tenth day he gives you the deadliest one of all, rabies so powerful it would kill an animal within a week, and then all you can do is wait. The days turn into weeks, the fever never comes, and you grow up spending your life guarding the gate of the first man ever to save a human from rabies.
