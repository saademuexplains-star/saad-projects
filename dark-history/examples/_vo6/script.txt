You're at the very bottom of the Titanic, shoveling coal in your underwear while rich people dance sixty feet above your head. At midnight, an iceberg rips the steel wall open, and freezing black water blasts into the room. Before you can run, a red warning bell rings—and the captain on the top deck flips a switch that drops heavy iron doors from the ceiling to trap the water down here with you. You sprint through chest-deep water and dive under the gap, but the heavy iron door comes down and pins your boot to the floor. You don't wait for help, because the ten men trapped behind you are clawing at your shirt to pull you back in. So you rip your bare foot out of your leather boot right as the door slams shut. Seconds later, the freezing ocean hits the red-hot coal furnaces behind that wall, and the boilers explode like a bomb, bending the steel door right behind your back. You limp up ten flights of iron stairs with one bleeding foot, thinking you're finally safe. You burst onto the freezing top deck and run toward a half-empty lifeboat—until an officer fires a warning shot into the air, points his gun at your chest, locks the iron gate so poor men can't take the rich people's lifeboats
