It's 1667, you're a madman wandering the streets of Paris, and the king's doctor decides to cure you by putting calf's blood into your veins. He thinks a gentle animal's blood will make you gentle too, so he pushes a silver tube into your arm, and warm animal blood pours in. Your arm burns. Pain tears through your back and your heart pounds, but he doesn't stop. That night your urine comes out black, and the whole house waits for you to die. But the next morning you're calm, quiet and clear-headed, and Paris calls it a miracle. Weeks later the madness comes back, so your wife drags you in for a second dose. The doctor hesitates. But your wife begs, and the silver tube slides back into your arm. Your body starts to shake and this time you die. The doctor is dragged into court, charged with murder... until the court finds out your wife had been feeding you arsenic the whole time.
